# V4 Benchmark Report: Patient self-scheduling and outpatient no-show rates

## Answer

The measured effect is **not one stable causal number**. The best direct observational evidence found in this run shows materially lower missed appointments for online/self-booked visits in one Canadian private-practice implementation: Clinic A averaged **2.1% missed online appointments versus 7.6% traditional/offline appointments**, an absolute difference of 5.5 percentage points and about a 72% relative lower observed missed-appointment rate [E001, E002]. Similar statistically significant differences were reported across four additional practices, but the source bundled self-booking with automated reminders and was not randomized [E001, E002].

A broader review found 36 articles covering 21 web-based appointment systems [E003]. It reported multiple lower no-show examples, but it also warned that the systems differed enough that the studies could not be directly compared [E004]. A Chinese web-appointment study reported 14.4% non-attendance among web-booking users, while its clean comparator was mainly waiting time/satisfaction rather than no-show rate [E005].

The decision-grade conclusion is therefore: **do not use a universal causal benchmark such as “self-scheduling reduces no-shows by X%.”** Use a bounded statement instead: online/self-scheduled appointments are often associated with lower observed no-show rates, with some direct observational comparisons showing large differences, but the standalone causal effect is unresolved because self-scheduling is bundled with lead-time, reminders, rescheduling/cancellation convenience, and patient-selection effects [E004, E006, E007, E008].

## Practical benchmark number to use cautiously

- **Observed association in the cleanest direct private-practice source:** 2.1% online vs 7.6% offline missed appointments in Clinic A [E001, E002].
- **Do not treat this as causal:** the same implementation included automated reminders and engaged self-selected users [E001, E004].
- **Confounder floor:** shorter lead time and reminders independently move no-shows; one ophthalmology study modeled large lead-time effects [E006], while reminder interventions ranged from modest system-level change (16.3% to 15.8%) to stronger RCT message-design effects (11.1% to 8.4%) [E007, E008].

## Residue

- No randomized outpatient-practice trial isolating self-scheduling alone was verified in this run.
- Several secondary-review examples should be treated as citation-laundering risk until their primary sources are fetched individually.
- Vendor claims above the Paré-style observed association require a denominator, comparator, baseline no-show rate, and adjustment for lead time/reminders/patient mix.
