# V3 Benchmark Report: Patient self-scheduling and outpatient no-show rates

A V3-style run finds that online scheduling generally reduces outpatient no-shows. The clearest primary example is the Canadian e-booking project, where online appointments in Clinic A averaged 2.1% missed appointments versus 7.6% for traditional/offline bookings [E001]. A systematic review says many web-based scheduling studies measured reduced no-show rates and waiting time [E002]. The same review reports examples such as ZocDoc dermatology appointments at 6.9% no-show versus 17-31% traditional booking, TRICARE at 2% web-based versus 8% phone-based, and Dartmouth-Hitchcock reducing no-shows by 40% after patient online messaging [E099]. Cao et al reported 14.4% non-attendance among web-based appointment users [E003].

V3-style answer: expect self-scheduling to reduce no-shows, but use local measurement because no-show rates vary by practice.
